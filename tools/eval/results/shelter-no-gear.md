> Even without tools or equipment, you can still create a basic windbreak shelter using natural materials.
> 
> 1. Find a large tree with a wide spread—sit close to its base or lean against it for immediate protection from wind and cold.  
> 2. Use branches, leaves, or grass to make a low barrier in front of you. Place them diagonally from the trunk outward to block wind.  
> 3. If possible, lie on your back and cover yourself with a blanket or layer of dry leaves and pine boughs (if available) to insulate against ground heat loss.  
> 4. Keep your body warm by tucking in your arms and legs—this reduces exposed skin and conserves body heat.  
> 
> Do not sleep directly on the ground. If you have no bedding, use a thin layer of dry grass or pine needles under you.
> 
> Stay alert: if you start feeling dizzy, cold to the touch, or shivering uncontrollably, move into better shelter immediately—this could be early signs of hypothermia.
> 
> Check your environment every 15 minutes. If it's still dark and no light is visible in the next 30 minutes, begin preparing for night-long survival: keep a source of warmth (like a small fire or heat pack) within reach.
> 
> Next step: if you see any signs of smoke, movement, or nearby lights, move toward them. Otherwise, stay safe, warm, and watch for changes in temperature or wind direction.
